# dotindot.in

The public website: a static site generated by one Node script (no framework, no database), served by the one dotindot app at `/`. Light theme only.

- **Design**: one typeface (Schibsted Grotesk), midnight ink on white, the brand orange used as the logo's full stop. A 12-column grid with fine rules instead of cards. Everything is made of dots: a dotted world map in the hero draws arcs from India to each market.
- **The scroll story (home)**: the full stop at the end of the headline swells until the screen is orange; a statement about the team lights up word by word; its own full stop grows into white (a dot in a dot); then "What we do" pins and one field of dots reshapes itself into a picture of each service as you scroll (the dots move away from the mouse). Plus a dot that trails the mouse, and pages that open out of the point you clicked (View Transitions, Chrome/Edge/Safari). With reduced motion it is a plain orange statement and a services grid.
- **Home**, in the order visitors ask: what we do (six services), who we work with (clients, industries, testimonials), why choose us (reasons, expertise, platforms, leadership), how we work, insights, questions and a contact form.
- **Navigation**: Home, Services (mega-menu), About, Insights, Contact, plus "Ask Dot" and "Get a free audit". Full-screen menu on phones. The Clients page is linked from the footer and the home page. "Client login" and "Employee login" are in the footer only.
- **Lead capture**: contact and audit forms, the website speed test (Google PageSpeed Insights) and the marketing health check, all posting into Sales → Pipeline with source, interest, budget in the visitor's currency, time zone and landing page. WhatsApp fallback if the CRM can't be reached.
- **Chat assistant**: Dot — the logo's D, whose hole is an eye that follows the pointer and blinks — sits in the corner (and behind every "Ask Dot" link). It opens with four starter questions and a free-audit shortcut, writes its replies word by word, turns links to our pages into buttons, and celebrates with a burst of dots when someone leaves their details. It answers from what the site says (using the AI provider in Settings → AI) and turns visitors into leads in Sales → Pipeline. Without an AI key, or if the AI is unavailable, it runs as a short guided conversation (need → name → email/WhatsApp), so leads are never lost. Conversations: Website → Chatbot.
- **Markets**: UK, Germany, the Netherlands, US, Canada, UAE, Singapore, Thailand, Japan, Australia and India — each with its own page, clocks and currency.
- **Smooth scrolling** with Lenis, restrained reveals below the fold, reduced motion respected, and everything works with JavaScript off.
- **Speed + SEO + AI search**: Lighthouse mobile 100 / 100 / 100 / 100, self-hosted font, per-page share images, and the full SEO set below.

## Edit → publish

The website is part of the one dotindot app: it is served at `/`, the employee & client app at `/app`, the API at `/api` (see `deploy/README.md` in the repo root).

**Most of the site is edited in the app: Growth → Website** (`/app/website`, permissions *Website — View / Edit & publish / Delete*; admins have them):
- *Content*: clients (logo + short description), testimonials, team, case studies, offices (address, phone, email, hours, map; shown on Contact, in the footer and in search-engine data) and certification/partner logos.
- *Insights*: articles in simple Markdown, with their own search title, description and share image; drafts stay off the site.
- *SEO*: Search Console / Bing verification, Google Analytics 4, and the title + description of every page.
- *Pages*: privacy policy, terms of use, llms.txt (automatic unless written by hand) and the "Who we work with" text.
- *Chatbot*: the assistant ("Dot") — on/off, name, first message, what it should know, AI provider/model, daily AI limit — and every conversation.
- *Contact & numbers*: key numbers, email, phone, WhatsApp, social links.

 Saving publishes: the app writes `content.json`, rebuilds the site next to the live one and swaps it in (a few seconds). Uploaded images go to `media/` (re-encoded to WebP, served at `/media/`). A testimonial shows only when it is marked *On website* and the client has agreed to be quoted. `content.json` and `media/` live only on the server (not in git); `deploy.sh` keeps them.

1. Edit copy in `src/content.mjs` and the defaults in `site.config.json` (the editor starts from these on first use, then takes over):
   - `email`, `phone`, `phoneDisplay`, `whatsapp` (digits only, with country code) — **the phone/WhatsApp numbers in the file are placeholders**.
   - `social` links (empty ones are hidden).
   - `clients`, `testimonials`, `caseStudies`, `team` — real ones only, with permission; empty lists stay hidden. Logos go in `assets/clients/`, photos in `assets/team/`.
   - `proof` — real numbers only; when three are filled they replace the three facts under the home headline.
   - `receipts.lighthouse` — the score shown on the home page. Re-run Lighthouse after big changes; clear it to hide that block.
   - `leadIntakeUrl` — already points at the app (`/api/intake/website`). (`employeeLogin` / `clientLogin` are kept for reference but not linked from the site.)
2. Try it locally: `node build.mjs` (Node 18+) → `dist/`. If `esbuild` is installed the script is minified.
3. Publish: push to git, then on the server `bash deploy/deploy.sh` (builds the website and the app, swaps them in, restarts).

Preview build for a static preview host: `PREVIEW=1 node build.mjs` (relative links, forms show a success message without sending, and Dot runs its guided conversation without the app).

## Search engines (once)

- Search Console / Bing: paste the HTML-tag verification values into `verify.google` / `verify.bing`, rebuild, upload, then submit `https://dotindot.in/sitemap.xml` in both.
- Optional: put a free Google Cloud API key (PageSpeed Insights API enabled) in `pageSpeedApiKey` so the speed test gets a bigger daily quota.
- Share images: `node og.mjs` (needs Playwright) re-renders `assets/og/*.jpg` and `assets/og.png` after you change titles; then rebuild.
- Every page ships with title, description, canonical, hreflang, Open Graph/Twitter cards with its own image, and JSON-LD (Organization, WebSite, WebPage with speakable, BreadcrumbList, Service, FAQPage, ItemList, Blog/BlogPosting, HowTo, WebApplication for the free tools). Also `sitemap.xml`, `robots.txt` (AI crawlers welcome), `llms.txt`, `llms-full.txt`, `humans.txt` and `.well-known/security.txt`.

## Files

- `build.mjs` — page templates, schema, sitemap, robots, llms.txt (and an `.htaccess` in case the site is ever hosted on Apache)
- `src/content.mjs` — all copy (services, why us, expertise, process, FAQs, roles, markets, health check)
- `src/insights.json`, `src/pages.json` — the starting articles and the built-in privacy/terms text (the editor takes over once used)
- `og.mjs` — renders the per-page share images (dev only)
- `src/styles.css` — design tokens and styles (inlined into every page)
- `src/site.js` — smooth scroll, header and menus, reveals, the hero map, the scroll story (full stop, statement, services reel of dots), the mouse dot, page transitions, testimonials, health check, speed test, clocks, currency, forms
- `content.json`, `media/` — written by the app's Website editor (server only, git-ignored)
- `site-index.json` — written by every build for the app: the page list (SEO), the generated llms.txt and the chat assistant's facts (git-ignored)
- `assets/fonts` — Schibsted Grotesk variable, Latin subset (SIL Open Font License)
- `assets/vendor` — Lenis (MIT) and the dotted world-map points
